## Findings

**The two model families catch different blockers.** Over the ten shipped cases there are 19
blocking defects. Counting a blocker as caught when either rep matched it, the Opus rerun catches
6 and gpt-6.1-sol 8, but only 3 are caught by both, so either one or the other catches 11. Only
gpt-6.1-sol matches the field extractor's two budget ceilings (`rs-1846`), one multi-select scope
defect (`rs-666` d2) and `rs-673` d3 and `rs-674` d3; only Opus matches the demo seed latch
(`rs-1297`) and `rs-673` d2 and `rs-674` d2. gpt-6.1-sol reports fewer findings outside the truth
file (1.6 per row against 2.8) but gets the verdict right less often (0.77 against 0.85): it
requests changes on the clean TSA refusal merge (`ra-1760`) on both reps, and on one rep each of
`rs-438` and `rs-499` it approves; like Opus, it requests changes on `rs-2079` on both reps.
Per-row grades are in the JSON.

**The shipped defects are harder than the pilot's planted ones.** On the pilot all but one row
matched the planted blocker. Here, reading the description its pinned head had, the read-only
reviewer matches under a third of the blockers that shipped (0.31) and 0.28 of all defects. It
misses both budget ceilings in the field extractor (`rs-1846`) and all four multi-select scope
defects (`rs-666`) on every rerun row. All three clean merges are approved on every row of all
three runs.

**The production path found nothing the read-only run missed.** On the seven cases both runs
graded, the L2 rows match the same blockers as the L1 rows (1 of 2, 1 of 2, 0 of 2 on the three
request-changes cases with blockers), at about eleven times the mean wall time per row. They
report twice as many findings outside the truth file (4.0 per row against 2.0 for L1 on the same
cases), 13 of the 28 on the three clean merges. At `high`, the dispatch row for `rs-1297`
did not finish in 30 minutes; the read-only rows found its blocker in about a minute.

**Stale descriptions inflated recall.** Each case pins the commit a reviewer saw, often an early
one, but the first two runs' fixtures carried the PR's final description, which describes later
commits, often including the fix for the defect the case scores. `7cfe2e3f` makes the builder
take the description as it read at the pinned head. Rebuilt that way, 14 of the first read-only
run's 17 findings on `PR_BODY` quote text that the description at the pinned head does not
contain, 14 of its 72 unmatched findings; against the rebuilt fixtures its claim rate is 0.85.

The rerun shows the final descriptions also led the reviewer to defects. Blocker recall falls from
0.48 to 0.31. Over the 18 case-rep pairs with blockers the paired difference is 0.16, with an
interval of 0.01 to 0.31, so the report marks it as a change, though it is smaller than the 0.20
noise floor the plan sets for 13 cases and 2 reps. Defect recall falls from 0.53 to 0.28. Five of
the seven lost matches are on cases whose final description covers the defect or its fix: final #438
says all 188 `.ts`, `.tsx`, `.mts` and `.cts` test files sit in exactly one project, final #2079
describes the `$line`-unset exit 0 and the guard that fixed it, and final #673 says the stale
last-capture record was fixed. Two first-run matches quote that text (one `rs-2079` row, one
`rs-673` row), and one `rs-438` row cites the coverage claim in a neighbouring finding; the other
two cite the code alone. The remaining two losses, one `rs-666` row and one `rs-674` row, came from
matches that cite the code alone, and a single row changing is inside rep-to-rep variation. Verdict
accuracy, 0.88 against 0.85, is within noise. The rerun reads 71% more input tokens per row and
takes 56% longer.

**`rs-2079` keeps its label.** Every row requests changes on the runbook case, and the first run's
rows call its one defect blocking: with `$line` unset, the content check prints `content OK` and
exits 0. The truth file says approve with that defect non-blocking, and the PR's own review record
supports it. The first pre-pass round approved the pinned head without the finding. The second rated
it advisory and pre-existing, since `main` printed the same line and exited 1 only because its last
command tested an absent PDF hash. The third round's blocking finding was a sentence in a later
description, not the code. The maintainer then chose the guard over correcting the sentence, and the
merged PR carries it. The miss is a disagreement about severity, scored as a wrong verdict. The
first run's rows matched the defect. Without the final description's account of it, the rerun's rows
match nothing and request changes on other findings.
