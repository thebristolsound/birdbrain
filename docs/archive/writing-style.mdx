This page is for contributors editing this docs site or the repository's Markdown. Birdbrain
adopted this standard on 2026-08-25 after a survey of the major documentation style guides; the
research brief with full citations is in the repository at
`docs/specs/2026-08-25-technical-writing-guides-brief.md`, and the contributor-facing original of
this page is `docs/agents/writing-guide.md` (which also covers audience, claim discipline, and
vocabulary — read it before a large docs change).

The standard has two layers. [Diataxis](https://diataxis.fr/) decides what kind of page you are
writing and the tone that follows from that. A mechanical rulebook drawn from the Microsoft,
Google, Kubernetes, and GitLab style guides governs sentences and formatting.

## Pick the page type first

Every page is one of four types, and the types do not mix within a page. Two questions place a
page: is the reader acting or trying to understand, and are they studying (acquiring skill) or
working (applying it)?

| The reader is | Studying    | Working      |
| ------------- | ----------- | ------------ |
| Acting        | Tutorial    | How-to guide |
| Understanding | Explanation | Reference    |

- **Tutorial** — a lesson. The writer owns the reader's success: every step visibly works and
  says what the reader will see. Keep inline explanation minimal and link out for depth. Warmth
  does real work here, because a learner who feels stupid stops.
- **How-to guide** — a competent user mid-task. The title names the goal as the user would phrase
  it. Assume competence, branch where reality branches, and keep the tone brisk and imperative.
- **Reference** — facts, structured to mirror the product, each fact stated once in a predictable
  place. No steps, no opinions. The mechanics below bind hardest here.
- **Explanation** — context and why. The only type where alternatives, history, trade-offs, and
  hedged language belong.

When an existing page resists improvement, the usual cause is content sitting in two cells at
once. Fix one page at a time rather than planning a grand reorganization.

## Mechanics

- **Voice.** Second person, active voice, present tense. Name the actor; passive only when the
  actor is unknown, irrelevant, or naming it would read as blame. No "there is" / "there are"
  openers.
- **Order.** Condition before instruction: "If the build fails, check the log." Prerequisites
  before step 1, never inside the steps. One action per step; a step may add one sentence of
  result.
- **Titles.** Sentence case, no end punctuation. Procedure titles start with an imperative, not a
  gerund — "Create a case", not "Creating a case".
- **Words.** The plain word over the fancy one ("use", not "utilize"; "because", not "since"
  unless time is meant). "For example" and "that is", not `e.g.` / `i.e.`. No idioms. No "easily"
  or "simply" — the reader who is stuck feels lied to. Expand an acronym on first use per page.
- **Inclusive terms.** Allowlist/denylist, main/replica, placeholder rather than dummy, and
  singular "they" for a hypothetical person — or rewrite to drop the pronoun.
- **Formatting.** Serial comma. American spelling. Bold for UI elements; code font for code,
  commands, filenames, and paths; italics for a new term on first use. Placeholders in angle
  brackets, defined in the surrounding text. Numbered lists only for sequences.
- **Links and images.** Link text describes the destination — never "click here". Every image has
  alt text; prefer text to a screenshot when either would do.

## Claim discipline

Birdbrain's readers include people documenting things that people with power would rather were not
documented, and a wrong claim about what the tool protects is not a cosmetic error. Two rules are
absolute:

- State what a mechanism proves *and* what it does not. A verified hash chain with signed (v2+)
  entries shows the manifest was not edited without the installation's signing key. It does not
  constrain the Operator, who holds that key and can mint an internally consistent chain; it does
  not cover legacy all-v1 manifests, whose entries predate signing and verify without one; and it
  does not show the captured page was genuine.
- No unqualified assurance words. "Tamper-proof", "court-admissible", "forensically sound", and
  "verified" never stand alone — name the standard and version, or describe the concrete property
  instead.
