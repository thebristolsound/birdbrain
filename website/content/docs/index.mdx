Birdbrain is an open-source desktop app with a companion Chromium extension for capturing,
organizing, and verifying web evidence. Captures include MHTML, a full-page screenshot, and
extracted page text, sent to the desktop app over `127.0.0.1` and stored locally in per-case
archives. There is no cloud account and no telemetry; captures stay on your machine unless you
export them.

Every capture is fingerprinted with SHA-256, timestamped, and chained to the previous capture in
a per-case manifest. You can verify the chain in the app, or export a self-contained HTML report
that is readable in any browser and verifiable without Birdbrain installed.

## Where to start

- **New to Birdbrain?** The [tester guide](./tester-guide.mdx) walks through installing the app and the
  extension and taking your first capture. The [screenshot tour](./screenshots.mdx) shows every screen.
- **Reviewing the security posture?** Start with the
  [architecture whitepaper](./birdbrain-architecture-whitepaper.mdx) for trust boundaries and the
  limits of the evidence claims, then the [threat model](./threat-model.mdx) for what the
  chain-of-custody controls do and do not defend against.
- **Working on the code?** The [implementation whitepaper](./birdbrain-technical-whitepaper.mdx) maps
  mechanisms to files, and the [capture pipeline](./capture-pipeline.mdx) reference covers the ingestion
  path end to end.
- **Adopting or contributing?** The
  [contributor and adoption whitepaper](./birdbrain-contributor-adoption-whitepaper.mdx) covers product
  fit, onboarding, and governance. The
  [privacy-focused adoption guide](./birdbrain-privacy-adoption-whitepaper.mdx) is written for
  activists, independent researchers, and small groups.

## Assurance baseline

Birdbrain measures itself against a standards register covering acquisition, preservation,
analysis, provenance, security, privacy, accessibility, and reporting, documented in
[OSINT investigation standards](./osint-investigation-standards.mdx). There is no universal
"OSINT-compliant" certification, so the project makes narrow, versioned, independently testable
claims instead.
